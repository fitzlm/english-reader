Every city has its own rhythm. In the early morning, before the traffic swells, you can hear the quiet persistence of street sweepers and the faint clatter of cafés rolling up their shutters. Urban planners call this the city’s metabolism — an intricate, largely invisible choreography that keeps millions of people fed, sheltered and moving.

When a storm or a blackout disrupts it, the most resilient neighborhoods are rarely the wealthiest. They are the ones where people know their neighbors, share what they have, and improvise together.
